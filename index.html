How this trip is paced

Kadi is recovering from surgery, so this plan is built around one anchor per day, door-to-door black cabs instead of walking, and a standing option to bail out and go back to the room. Everything is sequenced so the heaviest days sit in the middle, with a soft landing on arrival and an easy final morning.

The single luckiest thing about this trip: Sea Containers is a 4-minute stroll - or a £8 minimum-fare cab - from Tate Modern's front door. The biggest art day requires almost no travel, and you can retreat to the room between exhibitions.
And your Tate membership removes most of the pressure from Friday. Unlimited free entry to every exhibition, no booking, no timed slots - just walk in with the card. That means Friday isn't a schedule to keep, it's a menu. See one exhibition, go lie down, come back for another. Walk out of something halfway through and return Saturday evening. Nothing is forfeited, because nothing was paid for at the door.

Getting Around

Default to black cabs. Every London black cab is legally required to be wheelchair accessible - they have a low step-in, a swing-out ramp, and grab rails, which makes them far kinder than an Uber even when a chair isn't involved. Hail on the street, or use the Gett app to book one. The hotel doorman will have one waiting if you ask ten minutes ahead.

The Tube is the fallback, not the plan. Most central stations mean long tunnels, escalators and stairs. The one exception worth knowing: Blackfriars, a few minutes from the hotel, is fully step-free and useful if a cab is nowhere to be found.

Where to Eat

This list is built backwards from what Kadi actually eats: jacket potato, chips, pizza, cacio e pepe, fried chicken, chicken tenders or goujons, a cheese toasty, and a Caesar salad. Every place below was checked against its live menu and hours on July 27, and every one is a proper sit-down restaurant or pub that takes bookings. No chains, no counter service, no queueing.

One useful bit of translation: in Britain, chicken tenders are usually written as goujons or chicken strips, and a toasted cheese sandwich done properly is a croque monsieur. Worth knowing when reading a menu, because the thing she wants is often there under a name she wouldn't scan for.

This is the cheese toasty, done as it should be. Four croques on the all-day menu: Provencal £9.75, Monsieur £11.75, Madame £12.50, and one with goat's cheese at £13. A croque monsieur is a toasted cheese and ham sandwich under a French name, and it's the version worth crossing town for.
It's also the most accessible room of the whole trip, and that isn't a small thing this week. The restaurant is in a basement, but Zédel's own FAQ says the building is accessible from the street for wheelchair users, and so are the toilets. Euan's Guide, which reviews venues purely on disabled access, describes it as fully accessible via a lift. It's the only place on this list where step-free access is confirmed rather than something you have to ring up and hope for.
And it's a beautiful room for the money. A vast Art Deco hall off Piccadilly Circus with columns, mirrors and live music, where two courses cost £16.95. Jon gets flat-iron steak and frites at £27.50, haddock goujons with pommes frites at £23.25, or coq au vin at £25.50.
No pizza, no pasta, no jacket potato, no Caesar. This is a one-dish visit for her, and that dish is the croque. Worth it, but don't arrive expecting the rest of her list.

  • Angela's, Margate - 21 The Parade, CT9 1EX. Excellent seafood, daily catch menu, and literally nothing on Kadi's list. No pasta, no pizza, no chips, no jacket potato, two vegetarian dishes only.
  • Sargasso, Margate - no working website found and couldn't be confirmed as still trading.
  • The Magazine, Serpentine North - closed. Now Friends Of Ours, West Carriage Drive, W2 2AR. It is open, but recent reviews cite QR-only ordering, understaffing and waiting to be seated.
  • Padella Borough Market - right dish, wrong branch. Walk-in and virtual queue only.
  • The Wells, Hampstead - dining rooms are on the first floor, up stairs.
  • Rossopomodoro Covent Garden - appears to have closed; the site is a farewell notice.
  • Borough Market generally - the food is the best in London and the ground is the worst. Cobbles, crowds, standing room. Save it for a trip when walking is easy.
  • Lord Moon of the Mall, 16-18 Whitehall - closed. It would have been the obvious answer next door to the theatre; its pub page now sits in Wetherspoon's historical archive.
  • Popeyes, Wingstop and Slim Chickens - all three do the fried chicken and the tenders, and there's one inside Waterloo Station. Cut on your instruction: no fast food. Coqfighter is the sit-down version of the same craving.
  • Voodoo Chicken, Lower Marsh - very good fried chicken, but a market stall with no seating and lunchtime-only hours.
  • Melt Room, 26 Noel Street - Soho's grilled cheese specialist, and the obvious cheese toasty answer until you look closely. It's a counter-service sandwich shop rated 3.5 out of 5, no bookings. Café Zédel's croque is four minutes further and a different class of experience.
  • The Cheese Bar, Camden - the toastie restaurant is gone. It's now Pick & Cheese, a conveyor belt of cheese plates, which is a fun idea and not a cheese toasty.
  • The Cheese Barge, Paddington - a bookable restaurant on a boat with no toastie on the menu and no published step-free access. A 96-foot vessel is the wrong idea this particular week.
