How this trip is paced

This plan is built around one anchor per day, so there's room to linger, wander off the itinerary or head back to the room without feeling like you're missing anything. Cabs for the long hops, the tube for the short ones. The heaviest days sit in the middle, with a soft landing on arrival and an easy final morning.

The single luckiest thing about this trip: Sea Containers is a flat 8-10 minute walk along the Thames Path from Tate Modern's front door - or an £8 minimum-fare cab. The biggest art day needs almost no travel, and the room is right there between exhibitions.
And your Tate membership takes the pressure off Friday. Unlimited free entry to every exhibition, no booking, no timed slots - just walk in with the card. That means Friday isn't a schedule to keep, it's a menu: see one exhibition, break, come back for another, or return Saturday evening. Nothing is forfeited, because nothing was paid for at the door.
Cabs first, tube second One anchor per day Tate is drop-in - no bookings to miss Nothing before 10am except Margate & the flight home

Getting Around

Cabs for the long hops, tube for the short ones. A black cab is the right answer whenever the ride is more than a mile or two, or whenever the day has already been long. Hail one on the street, use the Gett app, or ask the hotel doorman ten minutes ahead and it'll be at the door. But two decent stations sit inside a ten-minute walk of the hotel, and on a couple of these routes the train genuinely beats sitting in traffic.

Blackfriars is the one worth knowing. It's the only station in London that spans the Thames, so there's an entrance on your side of the river - roughly five minutes east along the riverside, flat the whole way, lifts to every platform. That gives you the District and Circle lines plus Thameslink, which runs direct to St Pancras in 8 minutes for about £2-4, every few minutes all day.

Southwark is the other - about ten minutes on foot, lifts to the platforms, and it puts you on the Jubilee line, which reaches most of this week's destinations without a change. Waterloo is a twelve-minute walk, over the line you've drawn, so treat it as a station you arrive at rather than one you set out from.

Where to Eat

This list is built backwards from what Kadi actually eats: jacket potato, chips, pizza, cacio e pepe, fried chicken, chicken tenders or goujons, a cheese toasty, and a Caesar salad. Every place below was checked against its live menu and hours on July 27, and every one is a proper sit-down restaurant or pub that takes bookings. No chains, no counter service, no queue to order.

One useful bit of translation: in Britain, chicken tenders are usually written as goujons or chicken strips, and a toasted cheese sandwich done properly is a croque monsieur. Worth knowing when reading a menu, because the thing she wants is often there under a name she wouldn't scan for.

Bancone Borough Yards is the sit-down version. Arch 213, Stoney Street, SE1 9AD - a minute from the market, takes bookings, and does fresh pasta (the same group as the Kensington branch you're using Sunday). The room to book for a proper meal. Open Thu-Sat to 10:30 PM.
Padella's Borough original is here too - the cacio e pepe - but it's walk-in with a virtual queue and a long wait. Soho remains the branch to book. Treat a short Borough queue as luck, never the plan.

A proper English caff run by two Portuguese sisters, reportedly with fifteen-plus jacket potato fillings, on the pedestrianised stretch of Lower Marsh with tables outside. Reviews from this month confirm it's thriving. No website, just a Facebook page - which tells you what kind of place it is.
Lunch only, and that's the whole problem. It shuts at 2 PM. Thursday you'll be asleep, Saturday you're in Margate, Sunday you're in Hampstead, Monday you've flown. Friday around 12:30 is the single realistic window - slip out of Tate, five minutes each way, back before the Emin show.
Worth knowing: jacket potatoes have essentially vanished from London restaurant menus. Not one restaurant near the hotel, Whitehall, Hampstead or Kensington serves one. It's caff food now.

  • Angela's, Margate - 21 The Parade, CT9 1EX. Excellent seafood, daily catch menu, and literally nothing on Kadi's list. No pasta, no pizza, no chips, no jacket potato, two vegetarian dishes only.
  • Sargasso, Margate - no working website found and couldn't be confirmed as still trading.
  • The Magazine, Serpentine North - closed. Now Friends Of Ours, West Carriage Drive, W2 2AR. It is open, but recent reviews cite QR-only ordering, understaffing and waiting to be seated.
  • Padella Borough Market - right dish, wrong branch. Walk-in and virtual queue only.
  • Rossopomodoro Covent Garden - appears to have closed; the site is a farewell notice.
  • Borough Market, the full version - the food is the best in London and the ground is the worst: cobbles, crowds, and nowhere to sit down. Don't try to "do" it. The gentle hour on Thursday afternoon - cab there, one cheese toasty at Kappacasein, sit down, cab back - is the whole of it that's worth the energy on this trip. Anything more waits for a livelier visit.
  • Lord Moon of the Mall, 16-18 Whitehall - closed. It would have been the obvious answer next door to the theatre; its pub page now sits in Wetherspoon's historical archive.
  • Popeyes, Wingstop and Slim Chickens - all three do the fried chicken and the tenders, and there's one inside Waterloo Station. Cut on your instruction: no fast food. Coqfighter is the sit-down version of the same craving.
  • Voodoo Chicken, Lower Marsh - very good fried chicken, but a market stall with no seating and lunchtime-only hours.
  • Melt Room, 26 Noel Street - Soho's grilled cheese specialist, and the obvious cheese toasty answer until you look closely. It's a counter-service sandwich shop rated 3.5 out of 5, no bookings. Café Zédel's croque is four minutes further and a different class of experience.
  • The Cheese Bar, Camden - the toastie restaurant is gone. It's now Pick & Cheese, a conveyor belt of cheese plates, which is a fun idea and not a cheese toasty.
  • The Cheese Barge, Paddington - bookable, and a lovely idea, but there's no toastie on the menu and it's a 96-foot boat in Paddington Basin, which is a long way to go for cheese that isn't toasted.
