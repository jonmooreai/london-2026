How this trip is paced

Kadi is recovering from surgery, so this plan is built around one anchor per day, door-to-door black cabs instead of walking, and a standing option to bail out and go back to the room. Everything is sequenced so the heaviest days sit in the middle, with a soft landing on arrival and an easy final morning.

The single luckiest thing about this trip: Sea Containers is a 4-minute stroll — or a £8 minimum-fare cab — from Tate Modern's front door. The biggest art day requires almost no travel, and you can retreat to the room between exhibitions.
And your Tate membership removes most of the pressure from Friday. Unlimited free entry to every exhibition, no booking, no timed slots — just walk in with the card. That means Friday isn't a schedule to keep, it's a menu. See one exhibition, go lie down, come back for another. Walk out of something halfway through and return Saturday evening. Nothing is forfeited, because nothing was paid for at the door.

Day Two · Friday, August 7
Tate Modern, then Broadway on Whitehall
Big Day
9:00 AM
Breakfast at the hotel — complimentary
Eat properly. There is a lot of standing ahead, and Tate's cafés get slammed by noon.
10:00 AM2 hrs
Frida: The Making of an Icon
130 works plus documents, photographs and personal effects from her archives — the blockbuster of the year, and by far the busiest thing you'll see this week. Go at opening. Members walk straight in with no booking, so being there at 10:00 sharp means seeing it before the general-admission crowd builds.
Ask for a gallery stool at the entrance desk. Tate lends lightweight folding stools free of charge — it turns a hard 90-minute exhibition into a comfortable one. Most people don't know they exist. Also check tate.org.uk for Members Hours on Frida: Tate often runs early openings for Members before the doors open to everyone, which would be the quietest possible visit.
12:00 PM
The Members Bar — Level 5, Natalie Bell Building
This is your rest hub all day, and it's the best thing your membership buys on a trip like this. Quiet, seated, table service, and reached by lift from Level 4. Open 10:00–21:00 on Fridays, so it's there whenever you need to stop. Have lunch here rather than fighting for a table in the public cafés.
Alternative: the hotel room is 4 minutes away if you'd rather lie flat
1:45 PM75 min
Tracey Emin: A Second Life
Her largest exhibition ever — 90+ works across 40 years, including My Bed and Exorcism of the Last Painting I Ever Made. It charts her childhood in Margate through her illness and recovery, which lands differently the day before you go to Margate to see the studios she built there.
Closes August 30. This is your only window — it won't tour.
3:15 PM60 min
Ana Mendieta
Blavatnik Building, Level 2. Her biggest UK show ever — 120+ works, the Silueta series, newly remastered films, early paintings and late sculpture, much of it never shown in Britain. There's a tree sculpture from 1982 installed outside the building.
Running low? Walk out and come back. Membership means you can return Saturday evening (Tate is open late) or Sunday morning at no cost — and it runs to January 2027 regardless.

Day Three · Saturday, August 8
Margate — TEARS at TKE Studios
Day Trip
Saturday is the right day, and it's slightly lucky that it is. TKE Studios opens to the public only on Saturdays and Sundays, 12–5 PM, and only while an exhibition is running. The TEARS Final Show runs June 28 – August 31, so August 8 lands squarely inside it.
8:45 AM
Breakfast, then cab to St Pancras International
18–25 minutes, £18–25. Leave the hotel by 9:20. St Pancras is fully step-free (Category A) with accessible toilets on the circle concourse — the easiest big station in London.
10:00 AMapprox
Southeastern Highspeed to Margate
Roughly 1 hr 25 min to 1 hr 45 min via HS1. Book a specific train in advance for a cheaper fare and a reserved seat — do not wing it on a summer Saturday to a seaside town. Target something departing 10:00–10:30 to land comfortably before the noon opening.
Book Passenger Assist if you want help boarding — free, via Southeastern or National Rail, ideally 2+ hours ahead. They'll meet you at the platform and see you onto the train at both ends.
11:35 AM
Arrive Margate — taxi to the studios
TKE Studios is at 99 Victoria Road, CT9 1RD, roughly ¾ mile from the station and uphill from the seafront. There's a rank outside the station; it's a 5-minute, £6–9 ride. Don't walk this one.
12:00 PM90 min
TEARS Final Show — TKE Studios
The culminating exhibition of the Tracey Emin Artist Residency. Free entry, warm-white lighting, two floors. Coming here the day after her Tate retrospective is a genuinely good piece of trip design — the Tate show ends with Margate, and this is what Margate became.
Fully accessible, but ask on arrival. The front entrance has steps. There's a separate accessible entrance through the gates on the left side leading straight to a lift serving both floors and the basement. Tell the invigilator when you arrive and they'll walk you round. Accessible toilet has grab rails and emergency cords.
1:45 PM
Lunch by the harbour
Taxi down to the Old Town — Angela's and Sargasso on the harbour are the standouts, both small, both worth booking ahead for a summer Saturday.
Margate Old Town is cobbled and sloped. Take a cab between the studios, lunch and the station rather than linking them on foot — it's short distances but genuinely uneven ground.
3:15 PM
Optional: Turner Contemporary
Free, purpose-built, entirely step-free, and a two-minute taxi from the station — the easiest gallery you'll visit all week. Only if energy allows; the sea view from the café alone justifies it.
5:00 PMapprox
Train back to St Pancras
Home around 6:30–7:00 PM, then a cab to the hotel. Book dinner in the building tonight — after a day trip you will not want to go out again.
Day Four · Sunday, August 9
Freud in Hampstead, Cecily Brown in Kensington
Easy Pace
Why these two landed on Sunday. Friday was already Tate-plus-theatre, and stacking Serpentine on top of it would have meant crossing London twice in one day. Freud (Hampstead) and Serpentine (Kensington Gardens) sit on the same northwest axis, about 20 minutes apart, and both are open Sundays. It makes for a genuinely relaxed final full day.
10:30 AM
Cab to the Freud Museum
20 Maresfield Gardens, NW3 5SX — 30–40 minutes, £32–42. A long ride, but a beautiful one up through Regent's Park and Hampstead.
11:15 AM75 min
Freud Museum London
The house Freud fled to from Vienna in 1938 and died in a year later — his library, his antiquities, and the couch, all exactly as he left them. Anna Freud's rooms are upstairs. Open Wed–Sun, 10:30 AM – 5:00 PM.
Book a free Disabled Companion ticket. The museum offers one alongside a standard ticket. Ground floor is wheelchair accessible; if the first floor is too much, ask for the exhibition handout — they produce one specifically for visitors who can't manage the stairs. Access questions: +44 20 7435 2002.
12:45 PM
Lunch in Hampstead
The village is five minutes away by cab and full of good, unhurried Sunday lunch. Or hold out and eat at The Magazine beside Serpentine North.
2:30 PM75 min
Cecily Brown: Picture Making — Serpentine South
Free entry, no booking. Kensington Gardens, W2 3XA. Her first major solo painting show at a UK institution since Modern Art Oxford in 2005 — new and recent work made in response to the gallery's setting in the park, plus monotypes and drawings. Open Sundays 10 AM–7 PM.
Have the cab drop you as close to the gallery door as it can get. Serpentine South sits inside Kensington Gardens, and the default drop-off can leave you with a long path walk. Ask for West Carriage Drive, the closest vehicle access.

Getting Around

Default to black cabs. Every London black cab is legally required to be wheelchair accessible — they have a low step-in, a swing-out ramp, and grab rails, which makes them far kinder than an Uber even when a chair isn't involved. Hail on the street, or use the Gett app to book one. The hotel doorman will have one waiting if you ask ten minutes ahead.

The Tube is the fallback, not the plan. Most central stations mean long tunnels, escalators and stairs. The one exception worth knowing: Blackfriars, a few minutes from the hotel, is fully step-free and useful if a cab is nowhere to be found.
