How this trip is paced

This plan is built around one anchor per day, so there's room to linger, wander off the itinerary or head back to the room without feeling like you're missing anything. The tube and Thameslink do most of the getting-around - cheap, quick and frequent - with a cab kept for the airport, the late nights and the few spots the trains don't reach. The heaviest days sit in the middle, with a soft landing on arrival and an easy final morning.

The single luckiest thing about this trip: Sea Containers is a flat 8-10 minute walk along the Thames Path from Tate Modern's front door - or an £8 minimum-fare cab. The biggest art day needs almost no travel, and the room is right there between exhibitions.
And your Tate membership takes the pressure off Friday. Unlimited free entry to every exhibition, no booking, no timed slots - just walk in with the card. That means Friday isn't a schedule to keep, it's a menu: see one exhibition, break, come back for another, or return Saturday evening. Nothing is forfeited, because nothing was paid for at the door.
Tube first, cab when it earns it One anchor per day Tate is drop-in - no bookings to miss Nothing before 10am except Margate & the flight home

Day Four · Sunday, August 9
Freud & Kenwood in Hampstead, Cecily Brown in Kensington
Easy Pace
How the day flows. The morning stays in Hampstead: Freud first, then a five-minute cab up the hill to Kenwood House, which sits at the top of the Heath with the Iveagh Bequest hung inside for free - a Rembrandt, a Vermeer, a Gainsborough - and one of London's great terrace views. Lunch and Cecily Brown are down in Kensington, so the one long hop of the day comes after Kenwood, in the early afternoon, with the whole evening still in hand. Tube to the Freud Museum

11:15 AM75 min
Freud Museum London
The house Freud fled to from Vienna in 1938 and died in a year later - his library, his antiquities, and the couch, all exactly as he left them. Anna Freud's rooms are upstairs. Open Wed-Sun, 10:30 AM - 5:00 PM. Questions: +44 20 7435 2002.
12:45 PM~90 min
Kenwood House, at the top of the Heath
Hampstead Lane, NW3 7JR. A neoclassical villa on the northern edge of Hampstead Heath, and free to walk into - the Iveagh Bequest hangs on the walls: Rembrandt's late Self-Portrait with Two Circles, Vermeer's The Guitar Player, Gainsborough's Mary, Countess Howe, and works by Reynolds, Turner, Van Dyck and Constable. Robert Adam's barrel-vaulted library is the architectural set piece, and the terrace looks out over the Heath to the London skyline. Open daily 10 AM - 5 PM (last entry 4:30).
From Freud it's a short cab, ~15 min, £13-22 - the honest choice: no tube reaches Kenwood, and the bus doubles back to Golders Green to catch the one route (the 210) that runs back over the top, a 45-minute faff for a two-mile hop. If the legs are willing and the weather's kind, the walk up through Hampstead village and across the Heath is one of London's great ones (50-60 min, some hill and unpaved paths).
Entry's free, but book a timed slot anyway. English Heritage lets you reserve a free house ticket at english-heritage.org.uk and recommends it on a summer Sunday. The whole site is card-only. An hour and a half covers the house and the terrace view comfortably.
2:45 PM

This is the day's one long hop: from Kenwood down to Kensington is a cross-town cab, 30-45 min, £40-55 (Uber £30-45). It's the tired-legs, no-clean-train leg where the cab earns its keep - a bus-and-two-tubes route exists but runs about an hour with a change and a park walk at the end.

Getting Around

Take the train, keep a cab for when it earns it. Two decent stations sit inside a ten-minute walk of the hotel, contactless caps your daily spend automatically, and on most of these routes the train is both cheaper and quicker than sitting in traffic. A black cab is the right answer for the airport runs with all the luggage, the late night home after the theatre, and the two or three spots the trains simply don't reach - hail one on the street, use the Gett or Uber app, or ask the hotel doorman ten minutes ahead.

Blackfriars is the one worth knowing. It's the only station in London that spans the Thames, so there's an entrance on your side of the river - roughly five minutes east along the riverside, flat the whole way, lifts to every platform. That gives you the District and Circle lines plus Thameslink, which runs direct to St Pancras in 8 minutes for about £2-4, every few minutes all day.

Southwark is the other - about ten minutes on foot, lifts to the platforms, and it puts you on the Jubilee line, which reaches most of this week's destinations without a change. Waterloo is a twelve-minute walk, over the line you've drawn, so treat it as a station you arrive at rather than one you set out from.

Where to Eat

This list leans into the dishes you two are happiest to find on a menu: jacket potato, chips, pizza, cacio e pepe, fried chicken, chicken tenders or goujons, a cheese toasty, and a Caesar salad. Every place below was checked against its live menu and hours on July 27, and every one is a proper sit-down restaurant or pub that takes bookings. No chains, no counter service, no queue to order.

One useful bit of translation: in Britain, chicken tenders are usually written as goujons or chicken strips, and a toasted cheese sandwich done properly is a croque monsieur. Worth knowing when reading a menu, because the thing you're after is often there under a name you wouldn't scan for.
